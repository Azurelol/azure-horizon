# Opening

<p>This chapter covers the procedure for character creation in the system and other rules to keep in mind such as their advancement and equipment.</p>

# Wealth

<p>An individual character's wealth is not tracked by the system. Instead when they enter a settlement and purchase basic goods and services (such as resting in an inn) they are considered to be able to afford them.</p><p></p>

# Summary

<p>Characters are composed of the following:</p><ol><li><p><strong>Level</strong>: Up to level 99.</p></li><li><p><strong>Profile</strong>: Identity, Theme, Origin, Background.</p></li><li><p><strong>Attributes</strong>: These are the primary attributes for each character.</p></li><li><p><strong>Bonds</strong>: Mechanical relationships with other characters.</p></li><li><p><strong>Classes</strong>: Each level in a class provides a skill in that class.</p></li><li><p><strong>Equipment</strong>: Each character can equip weapons, armor and accessories.</p></li></ol>

# Parameters

<p>The following parameters are derived from a character’s attributes and can be modified through skills, equipment and various effects.</p><h3>Shared Resources</h3><ul><li><p>Hit Points (HP): Represents a character’s capability to fight. When reduced to 0, the character is defeated and at the mercy of their opponent.</p></li><li><p>Mind Points (MP): Represents the character’s internal, finite reserves of willpower. Required for all spells and some skills.</p></li><li><p>Block: (BLK): Temporary hit points granted by certain actions.</p></li></ul><h3>Hero Resources</h3><ul><li><p>Tension Points (TP): Represents the character’s current mental state or wellness. Ranges from 0 to 10.</p></li><li><p>Inventory Points (IP): Represents the character’s access to consumables. Expended in order to produce consumables for use during scenes. </p></li></ul>

# Classes

<p>A character's classes inform not just their background and aspirations but also their current state in the campaign. They are designed with the following design principles.</p><h2>Design Principles</h2><ul><li><p>The core set of classes should cover most of the classical archetypes.</p></li><li><p>Most classes should provide an anchor skill that significantly alters the PC’s tactics and can serve as the foundation for the combat style for a character. (Like the AM’s stances).</p></li><li><p>Each class should have at least one non-combat-oriented skill.</p></li><li><p>No skills should be “splashable”, as in being powerful 1-point investments into the class.</p></li><li><p>Avoid the classical splatbook powercreep of newer options over older ones. Instead, aim to fulfill class/character fantasies and gameplay loops.</p></li><li><p>Talents should not provide combat bonuses. Instead they should reinforce the flavor of the class when used outside combat.</p></li><li><p>Given a standard party of 4 PCs, there should be minimal overlap between classes. For the purposes of the design an average of 2 shared classes between 2 PCs is ideal.</p></li></ul><h2>Summary</h2><p>Each class is a composed of the following:</p><div><table><tbody><tr><td><p>Level</p></td><td><p>The level of a class is equal to the number of skill points invested in skills that belong to it.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Traits</p></td><td><p>A set of key words which can be used to determine whether a character has access to certain skills, spells, equipment, items.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Skills</p></td><td><p>Each class comes with 5 skills that can be selected.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Mastery</p></td><td><p>Once a class has 10 class levels, it is considered mastered. This provides an unique bonus for it.</p></td></tr></tbody></table></div><h2>Progression</h2><ul><li><p>The character gains class levels every odd level starting from level 1 to level 60.</p></li><li><p>Each class can only have up to 10 skill points invested in it.</p></li><li><p>A PC can have a max of 2 unmastered classes at any time.</p></li><li><p>A level 99 PC would have 3 of their core classes and the legendary <em>Hero</em> class starting from level 60 onwards.</p></li><li><p>Multiple instances of a trait stack in terms of the bonuses they can give upon a roll. (Such as a hero having two Warrior classes).</p></li></ul>

# Equipment

<h2>Design Principles</h2><ul><li><p>There should be clear strengths and drawbacks between equipment choices. There shouldn’t be a clear choice for a given archetype.</p></li><li><p>Accessories should neither give always-applicable global numerical bonuses or provide immunities to status effects. Instead bonuses should be conditional when given and resistances to status effects should cost something.</p></li></ul><p>Some equipment has certain trait restrictions, meaning it can only be used by characters with those tags.</p><h3>Slots</h3><p>Each character can equip items in the following slots:</p><ul style="margin-top:0;margin-bottom:0;padding-inline-start:48px" id="docs-internal-guid-b3ef2203-7fff-8b00-0290-53308133287b"><li><p>Main Hand</p></li><li><p>Off Hand</p></li><li><p>Armor</p></li><li><p>Accessory 1</p></li><li><p>Accessory 2</p></li></ul><p>Each piece of equipment is composed of its attributes and traits.</p><h3>Engrams</h3><p>Depending on the campaign setting, these are pieces of equipment which grant the user access to specific spells or magic.</p><ul><li><p>Each engram has a list of required traits the character must have in order to equip it.</p><p>They can be changed during rest scenes.</p><p>They are commodities and can be purchased, sold and discovered during play.</p><p>Engrams of higher rarity can grant multiple spells.</p></li></ul>

# Tension

<p>Tension represents a player character’s mental state and capacity to endure hardships. Higher levels of tension are generally detrimental to a character’s wellbeing (though some skills benefit from doing so).</p><p>A well-rested character is at 0 tension, which can increase over the course of an adventure due to external factors such as stress, encountering evil, experiencing hardships. Certain adversary skills can also directly increase tension during combat.</p><p>Here are the general cases where tension increases:</p><ul><li><p>Using a skill. Some skills also generate more tension (such as those from the Berserker while raging).</p></li><li><p>Entering crisis.</p></li><li><p>Witnessing a party member being defeated.</p></li><li><p>Ending combat with over half the party in crisis.</p></li></ul><p>When a character reaches max tension, they must make a tension check.</p><ul><li><p>On success, they gain Conviction and gain hit points and mind points.</p></li><li><p>On failure, they suffer from Stress and lose mind points and focus.</p></li></ul>

# Creation

<p>The procedure for creating a new character for a campaign is something the everyone at the player should at least start together on session zero, leaving only the minor details out until the prologue starts.</p><p>It will take place over a sequence of steps, (although the order of some steps can be changed around).</p><p>Each hero generally starts play at level 10.</p><ol><li><p>Create the hero's <strong>profile</strong>, which is a set of descriptors which are used to frame who the character is and why they are taking part in the campaign</p></li><li><p>Choose the hero's initial <strong>classes</strong>, up to a maximum of two.</p></li><li><p>Decide on the hero's initial <strong>attributes</strong> among the four attribute scores.</p></li><li><p>Pick out the hero's starting <strong>equipment</strong>.</p></li><li><p>Decide on the hero's <strong>name</strong> and <strong>artwork</strong>.</p></li></ol>

# Ideals

<p>What makes a not-so ordinary character a hero? This system believes that a hero must hold some of the following ideals.</p><ul><li><p>They are proactive towards realizing their goals.</p></li><li><p>They would rather sacrifice themselves than others.</p></li><li><p>They empathize towards others, even their enemies.</p></li><li><p>They will take responsibility for what they have done.</p></li><li><p>They can't stand to suffering or injustice.</p></li><li><p>They long to find a place to belong..</p></li><li><p>They regret their mistakes.</p></li><li><p>They will fight oppression whenever they can.</p></li><li><p>They want to create a place for others to belong.</p></li><li><p>They will unite instead of divide, bringing people together.</p></li></ul><p>In short, a hero could start the campaign as a troubled mercenary, an ex-assassin or someone who was capable of terrible things in their past but is now committed to putting that behind them.</p><p>They crystallize the ideals we not-so-fictional people would like to aspire to but find difficult to put into practice.</p>

# Profile

<p>A character’s profile is the starting point of their fiction and is authored by players at the start of the campaign. It provides both player and the director with narrative hooks that can be explored during the course of a campaign.</p><ol><li><p>Identity — How the character sees themselves. E.g. "Retired Assassin."</p></li><li><p>Theme — Why the character fights. E.g. "Vengeance."</p></li><li><p>Origin — Where the character comes from. E.g. "Imperial Capital Slums."</p></li><li><p>Anchor — What the character cannot bear to lose. Their emotional anchor. E.g. "Dog."</p></li></ol><p>While the focus of a campaign should be the resolution of the greater threats and problems that endanger the world the player characters live in, the director is encouraged to thread each character’s storylines into the greater narrative as seamlessly as possible.</p><p>As such, players are encouraged to author characters whose inclusion fit the setting, enrich the shared fiction and have personal goals that match the party’s. Their characters should be serving the narrative and their portrayal should enrich the table’s experience with it.</p>

# Attributes

<p>Each PC and NPC has the following attributes which provide an abstract representation. </p><ul><li><p>Might (MIG): Represents a character’s overall physical strength and ability to withstand punishment.</p></li><li><p>Dexterity (DEX): Represents a character’s overall reflexes and coordination, ability to perform tasks with precision.</p></li><li><p>Insight (INS): Represents a character’s overall mental acuity, ability to perceive things and reason out logical deductions.</p></li><li><p>Willpower (WLP): Represents a character’s willpower and emotional resilience.</p></li></ul><p>Each attribute is represented by a die size, ranging from d4 to d12.</p><p>A character starts at level 10 with one of the following attribute arrays.</p><ul><li><p>d8,d6,d4,d4</p></li><li><p>d6,d6,d6,d4</p></li></ul>

# Level

<p>Each player character starts play at level 10 and during the course of play can reach a maximum level of 99. A character will level at least once per session, sometimes two if they meet many experience triggers during the credits sequence.</p><p>Experienced groups may begin play at higher tiers.</p>
