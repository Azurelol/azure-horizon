# Checks

<h2>Action Checks</h2><p>This type of Check is used when a character performs various actions such as attacks, skills and spells. Its result determines how potent the action was, in various tiers. Any actions that lead to this check cannot miss. Their consequences (such as taking damage) can only be avoided or diminished through the use of specific defensive actions.</p><p>Depending on the result of the check, the potency of the action varies.</p><div><table><tbody><tr><td><p>Failure</p></td><td><p>Reduced</p></td><td><p>Usually minimal damage with no extra effects.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Success</p></td><td><p>Standard</p></td><td><p>Average damage with default effects.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Critical</p></td><td><p>Powerful</p></td><td><p>Same as standard, provides an opportunity.</p></td></tr></tbody></table></div><h3>Examples</h3><p>Meravel attacks a Wolf which has 8 DEF.</p><div><table><tbody><tr><td><p>13</p></td><td><p>Success</p></td><td><p>The wolf suffers the sword’s (base bonus + high roll) in physical damage.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>7</p></td><td><p>Failure</p></td><td><p>The wolf suffers (base bonus * 0.5) in physical damage.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>16</p></td><td><p>Critical Success</p></td><td><p>The wolf suffers (base bonus + high roll) and the character gets an opportunity. CB is a valid opportunity option.</p></td></tr></tbody></table></div><p></p><h2>Opposed Checks</h2><p>This type of Check is performed when two characters oppose each other in an action.</p><h2>Recovery Checks</h2><p>Some harmful effects persist until a successful recovery check has been made.</p>

# Rest

<h2>Short Rest</h2><p>During short rest scenes, for every 2 PCs in the party one of them can perform an activity.</p><p>You should rotate among yourselves and avoid consecutive repeats.</p><h2>Long Rest</h2><p>We call the times when the heroes retire for the day at a safe location (such as a settlement or at camp) <em>long rests.</em> In these locations the heroes are able to restock their supplies, unwind and fully prepare for their next day.</p><p>During long rest scenes, each PC <em>fully recovers</em> their hit points, mind points, inventory points and clears all their tension points.</p>

# Downtime

<p>When the heroes are in settlement or exploring a location they may want to do certain actions whose resolution should be simple and painless.</p><p>These will be covered in this section.</p><h2>Gathering Information</h2><p>When the players want to spend time gaining information about people, places or things they can make a @CHECK[ins wlp].</p>

# Journey

<p>We define a <em>Journey</em> in the system as the activity performed when the party travels from their current location to a distant one over the course of one day or more.</p><p>When there is risk involved, we call that a <em>journey</em> and make use of the rules in this section. Each journey will begin with a <em>travel check</em>.</p><p></p><h2>Travel Check</h2><p>Once the heroes have decided on a destination and have confirmed they are setting out, the party will make a travel check. A player can volunteer one of their heroes to make the check using the dice listed below.</p><table><tbody><tr><td><p>Location</p></td><td><p>Danger Level</p></td><td><p>Travel Check</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Settlements</p></td><td><p>Low</p></td><td><p>D4</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Road</p></td><td><p>Moderate</p></td><td><p>D6</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Wilderness</p></td><td><p>High</p></td><td><p>D8</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Remote Locale</p></td><td><p>Severe</p></td><td><p>D10</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Hazardous Locale</p></td><td><p>Extreme</p></td><td><p>D12</p></td></tr></tbody></table><ul><li><p><strong>On a result of 6 or higher</strong>, the party encounters a danger.</p></li><li><p><strong>On a result of 1</strong>, the party makes a discovery.</p></li></ul><p>After the check and its resulting event is resolved, the party will enter a long rest scene at their <em>Camp</em>. Here the heroes can unwind, work on their downtime projects and deepen their relationships.</p><blockquote><p>Most journeys can be resolved with a single travel check. Refrain from making more than that unless it's a very long journey lest you bog down your session.</p></blockquote><h2>Dangers</h2><h3>Hazard</h3><p>The party can encounter a natural hazard, which will be taxing to traverse.</p><h3>Ambush</h3><p>The party can be beset upon by hostile creatures.</p><h3>Loss</h3><p>The party loses an important item or a piece of equipment. Let the players volunteer it.</p><h3>Villain</h3><p>The party can encounter a villain or one of their associates.</p><p></p><h2>Discoveries</h2><p>One of the most positive outcomes of a travel check is that of a discovery, which can be one of the following.</p><h3>Location</h3><p>An entrance to an abandoned keep. A waterfall with a hidden entrance behind it. All of these are locations where exciting developments can occur and where danger lurks. A chance for the heroes to test themselves and learn more about the world.</p><h3>Guests</h3><p>There are many individuals of an adventurous streak who make a living in the world. When you encounter one of them and yours goals align perhaps they can join your party for a time. Maybe they need help. Maybe you are the ones in need of help. (See the rules for <em>Guests</em>).</p><h3>Merchant</h3><p>It is not uncommon for enterprising merchants to travel the lands, especially while making use of roads or when traveling to settlements. In such encounters perhaps such merchants can offer the party some rare items or consumables?</p><h3>Treasure</h3><p>Heroes may uncover treasure by chance while traveling through an abandoned area. Such treasure can be a rare ingredient or material, a piece of equipment or perhaps a plot-relevant item that leads to heroes gathering more information.</p><h3>Vista</h3><p>During travel one of the possible discoveries is that of a vista. A breathtaking locale or solemn monument. In short, they are memorable places of your party's journey.</p><p>When encountering one, the players are incentivized to take the lead in describing it and how it fits with the setting.</p><p>If one of the players decides to contribute art for it, they get bonus points! You will want to save it in your <em>album.</em></p>

# Rituals

<p>A ritual is an activity performed whenever a hero with a magical aptitude wants to perform a magical feat outside of a battle.</p><p>If a spiritist wants to free a tormented ghost or if an elementalist wants to pacify rampant elementals in a location, this is the activity they would perform in the system.</p><p>They are meant to be straightforward procedures which parameters are a conversation between the ritual caster and the director.</p><p>First the player states if they could perhaps do X using a ritual in order to achieve an outcome they desire. They make an argument in good faith based on their character's traits and how they could reasonably be capable.</p><p>The director then deliberates on whether a ritual would suitable to the situation If so, they then coordinate with the player to set the <em>Potency</em> and the <em>Area </em>of the ritual, the factors which determine its difficultyLevel and cost in mind points.</p><h2>Parameters</h2><p>Traits</p><p>The character who wishes to perform the ritual must have traits that would be relevant to it. For example a ritual where the character wants to help a spirit move on would require the Light trait that spiritists get. Perhaps <em>Dark</em> if the player in question makes a compelling argument for it.</p><h3>Potency</h3><p>The Potency affects the difficultyLevel and base cost of the ritual. The more difficult something is to achieve the higher the potency required of the ritual will be.</p><table><tbody><tr><td><p></p></td><td><p>Cost</p></td><td><p>Difficulty</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Easy</p></td><td><p>30</p></td><td><p>7</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Normal</p></td><td><p>40</p></td><td><p>10</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Hard</p></td><td><p>50</p></td><td><p>13</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Impossible</p></td><td><p>50</p></td><td><p>16</p></td></tr></tbody></table><h3>Area</h3><p>The Area affects the scope of the ritual. Whether limited to a single object or a whole room and bigger besides.</p><table><tbody><tr><td><p></p></td><td><p>Factor</p></td><td><p>Example</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Entity</p></td><td><p>1</p></td><td><p>A single person or object.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Small</p></td><td><p>2</p></td><td><p>A small room.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Large</p></td><td><p>3</p></td><td><p>A chamber.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Huge</p></td><td><p>4</p></td><td><p>A soccerball field.</p></td></tr></tbody></table><h2>Outcome</h2><p>A ritual can have four distinct outcomes based on the result of the check.</p><table><tbody><tr><td><p></p></td><td><p>Outcome</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Critical</p></td><td><p>They get what they hoped for and more besides.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Success</p></td><td><p>They get what they want.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Partial</p></td><td><p>They mostly get what they want but there are minor complications.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Fumble</p></td><td><p>Something went terribly wrong and there are major complications.</p></td></tr></tbody></table><p>Based on the outcome, the director will decide what happens.</p><blockquote><p>When it comes to coming up with a complication on the spot it can be a good idea to bring the players into coming up with an interesting one. Having a conversation about it can help.</p></blockquote><p>As always, complications that arise from rituals must be interesting and add to the narrative.</p><h2>Samples</h2><p>The following are examples of some rituals players may want to perform during play.</p>
