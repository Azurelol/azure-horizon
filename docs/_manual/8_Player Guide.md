# Basics

<h2>Dissapointment</h2><p>Sometimes things will not go your way. When that happens, it’s best to play into it and handle it gracefully. Remember! Your GM does not want you to fail and will most likely feel bad when you do.</p><p>Even if you are momentarily disappointed, you need to communicate to the rest of the table that you are okay with this. In this way you contribute to an atmosphere of play where failure is acceptable, and that it should lead to interesting outcomes.</p><h2>Fishing</h2><p>Don’t be a weasel!</p><p>It is understandable and expected of you to want to make use of your player character’s traits and talents as much as possible. It is not okay to want to shoehorn them everywhere and argue in essentially bad faith to the director to fish for bonuses. It is not being clever.</p><p>It is annoying and puts your GM in a bad spot.</p><p>Will your muscle-bound bruiser who hasn’t shown competency in ancient languages be bad at a check to understand what some ancient mummy just said to the party? Yes, they should be. And they will most likely and should fail at doing so.</p>
