---
title: Guiding Principles
---

<p>This game system has been implemented with the following principles in mind:</p><ul><li><p>Every system and rule must justify its place in ink. </p></li><li><p>Complexity must be minimized wherever possible.</p></li><li><p>Design language should use resonance as much as possible. While some key words may need to be flavored after the system aesthetic, make that the exception rather than the norm.</p></li><li><p>No design survives first contact. The system must be able to evolve and be improved upon user and internal feedback after release.</p></li><li><p>The rules should be intuitive when possible and clearly explained when they cannot.</p></li><li><p>Not everything needs to be made a mechanic out of. Only the things the system cares about should be.</p></li><li><p>Defeat of the player characters is not the end of a campaign, but an opportunity for interesting consequences and a shift in the narrative.</p></li><li><p>Combat is treated as entertainment and not as a sport or war. While it certainly favors the players side, defeat can happen and should be handled gracefully.</p></li><li><p>Balance is a nice goal to have but not at the cost of homogeneity.</p></li><li><p>No character option should eclipse all others in its space so that players would feel compelled to take it every time without question.</p></li><li><p>The system should provide flavoring only as much as is needed to inspire GMs to come up with their own settings.</p></li><li><p>Avoid the use of currency in discrete terms and instead think of it in terms of abstract wealth for the party.</p></li><li><p>No crafting rules.</p></li></ul>
